# Actual coupled normal branches: centered soft limits and flat DOS

**PROVED.** In a finite angular system with matched reciprocal retarded kernels, the sharp scalar coefficient \(C_\infty\) holds at arbitrary boson energies for energy-independent DOS. It also holds for sufficiently wide finite centered boxes at fixed physical glue, and as a sharp asymptotic onset bound under a common soft scaling with arbitrary centered, possibly varying, channel DOS families. Equal angular branches are not assumed. The optimal coefficient for general energy-dependent branches remains open.

## 1. Model and statement

Take a fixed finite channel set with positive DOS-height measures \(\eta_x\). Let \(d\mu_{xy}(t)=d\mu_{yx}(t)\ge0\), supported on \(0<t<\infty\), with both \(J_{xy}=\int d\mu_{xy}\) and \(L_{xy}=\int t^{-1}d\mu_{xy}\) finite. The variables here are dimensionless: at a test energy \(\tau=k_BT>0\), \(d\mu\) is the physical glue moment divided by \((2\pi\tau)^2\), and each physical mode has \(E^2/(2\pi\tau)^2=\epsilon t\), where \(\epsilon>0\). Thus
\[
 K_j^\epsilon(x,y)=\int\frac{d\mu_{xy}(t)}{j^2+\epsilon t},\qquad K_0^\epsilon=L/\epsilon,
 \qquad 0\le K_j^\epsilon\le J/j^2,\quad K_j^\epsilon\longrightarrow J/j^2\quad(j\ge1).
\]
Matrices act in the \(\eta\)-measure, for example \((Lz)_x=\sum_y\eta_yL_{xy}z_y\). Set \(c=\|J\|_{L^2(\eta)}\). In particular, \(L_{xy}>0\) if and only if \(J_{xy}>0\). This is a fixed graph and a fixed inverse moment before softening; arbitrarily different scalings of extra modes or varying angular graphs are not included.

At each \(\epsilon\), let each dimensionless radial DOS \(r_x^\epsilon(u)\) be measurable, integrable, even, and between 0 and 1. The choice of centering fixes the real part of the normal state to zero; no frequency-dependent real shifts are allowed in this result. Write \(w_n=2n+1\), \(P_{nm}^\epsilon=K_{|n-m|}^\epsilon-K_{n+m+1}^\epsilon\ge0\), \(H_{nm}^\epsilon=K_{n+m+1}^\epsilon\), and \(S=P+2H\). On the actual centered reflected branch,
\[
 s_{n,x}=w_n+\sum_{y,m\ge0}\eta_y P_{nm}^\epsilon(x,y)b_{m,y},\qquad
 b_{n,x}=\frac1\pi\int \frac{s_{n,x}}{s_{n,x}^2+u^2}r_x^\epsilon(u)\,du.
\]
Only the matched retarded interaction is used. A pairing-only PSD subtraction can only improve the bounds. If an angular site has exactly zero DOS at a given \(\epsilon\), its Cooper coordinates are deleted.

For even frequency, let \(g_{n,x}=\sqrt{w_n b_{n,x}}v_{n,x}\) and use the Hilbert norm \(\|g\|^2=\sum_{x,n}\eta_x|g_{n,x}|^2\). With \(\mathscr H^\epsilon\) the angular-frequency operator of kernel \(H_{nm}^\epsilon(x,y)\sqrt{b_{n,x}b_{m,y}/(w_nw_m)}\), the stability form is
\[
 Q_\epsilon(g)=\|g\|^2+\frac12\sum_{x,y,n,m}\eta_x\eta_y P_{nm}^\epsilon(x,y)b_{n,x}b_{m,y}|v_{n,x}-v_{m,y}|^2-2\langle g,\mathscr H^\epsilon g\rangle.
 \tag{1}
\]
Expand the square, use reciprocity and the normal equation, and use \(S=P+2H\). The ordered-pair factor is \(1/2\); both nonzero reflection contributions give the factor 2 on \(H\). The original even gap form is positively congruent to (1). The even matrix with nonnegative entries also dominates the full-frequency pairing spectral top by folding absolute values, before any PSD subtraction.

Let \(\kappa=(2\pi C_\infty)^2>0\) denote the exact top of the scalar flat soft operator with transfers \(1/j^2\), \(j\ge1\), as in the companion finite-band manuscript, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.24364879](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24364879). In particular \(\kappa\ge1\) by its first diagonal. If \(a_\epsilon=\inf_{\|g\|=1}Q_\epsilon(g)\), with finite-support tests and \(a_\epsilon=1\) for an empty Cooper space, then
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle \liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow0} a_\epsilon\ge 1-\kappa c.}\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, at any fixed test temperature \(\tau>C_\infty\sqrt{\rho}\), with physical moment norm \(\rho=(2\pi\tau)^2 c\), there is a common sufficiently soft range of strict stability over the arbitrary centered DOS choices above. This does not exclude excess tending to zero with \(\epsilon\) at the nominal threshold. The coefficient for this limit cannot be lowered: include the one-channel wide-box soft sequence from the companion theorem (or use the box limit below with scaled halfwidth tending to infinity).

**Uniform onset consequence.** Fix the physical reciprocal moment measures in the variable \(E_0>0\), with finite \(E_0^{-2}\) integrals on every edge, and replace their energies by \(\sqrt{\epsilon}E_0\) without changing their weights. Write \(\tau_{\rm lin}^\epsilon=k_BT_{\rm lin}^\epsilon\). Then \(\limsup_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\sup_{\{r_x^\epsilon\}}\tau_{\rm lin}^\epsilon\le C_\infty\sqrt{\rho}\). Otherwise some \(\epsilon_i\to0\) have negative forms at \(\tau_i\ge C_\infty\sqrt{\rho}+\delta\) for a fixed \(\delta>0\). The general bound in [SHARP-Q2](../SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md) keeps \(\tau_i\le\sqrt{\rho}/(\pi\sqrt2)\); extract a subsequence tending to \(\tau_*>0\). At these temperatures \(K_0=(1/\epsilon_i)\int E_0^{-2}\,\mathrm d\mathfrak m\) has the same fixed inverse moment in the scaled equation, and each \(K_j\) at \(j\ge1\) tends to \(J_*/j^2\), with \(J_*=\int\mathrm d\mathfrak m/(2\pi\tau_*)^2\). The dominating bound uses the positive lower bound on \(\tau_i\), uniformly in \(i\). The compactness, locking and residual estimates below therefore apply unchanged on this sequence, giving a positive form liminf \(1-\kappa\rho/(2\pi\tau_*)^2\), a contradiction. The dimensionless DOS may depend on \(\tau_i\); this is permitted because the proof allows arbitrary varying profiles.

## 2. The scaled centered equation and the active components

The centered branch exists uniquely at each \(\epsilon>0\). The row of \(P\) sums in frequency to \(K_0+2\sum_{j=1}^nK_j\) by telescoping. Let \(R\) be the maximum angular integral of \(K_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}K_j\); it is finite for fixed \(\epsilon\). The normal map preserves \(w_n\le s_{n,x}\le w_n+R\). A Poisson average \(B(s)\) of a nonnegative DOS obeys \(|sB'(s)|\le B(s)\), by differentiating its kernel. On the invariant box the log-coordinate derivative of the normal row has sum of absolute values at most \(R/(1+R)\). To see this, the derivative numerator is bounded by the normal contribution \(\sum\eta_yP b\), not by the bare term; divide by the full positive row \(w_n+\sum\eta_yP b\). The log-coordinate box is complete in the supremum metric relative to \(\log w_n\). The contraction also applies if a DOS vanishes identically. No Matsubara cutoff is used.

Write \(z_{n,x}^\epsilon=\epsilon s_{n,x}\), set \(\widetilde r_x^\epsilon(u)=r_x^\epsilon(u/\epsilon)\), and define \(f_x^\epsilon\) as the Poisson average of \(\widetilde r_x^\epsilon\). Then \(b_{n,x}=f_x^\epsilon(z_{n,x}^\epsilon)\). For each fixed \(n\),
\[
 z_{n,x}^\epsilon=(Lb_n)_x+O_n(\epsilon).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the diagonal frequency coefficient is \(L/\epsilon-K_{w_n}^\epsilon\), the other coefficients are bounded by the summable nonzero transfers, and the bare term contributes \(\epsilon w_n\). Also, without dropping its sign, the normal equation gives
\[
 z_{n,x}^\epsilon\ \ge\ \sum_y\eta_y\bigl(L_{xy}-\epsilon K_{w_n}^\epsilon(x,y)\bigr)b_{n,y}.
 \tag{4}
\]
All estimate constants are independent of the choice of DOS.

On any sequence \(\epsilon\to0\), extract a subsequence on which \(\widetilde r_x^\epsilon\) converges weak-* in \(L^\infty(\mathbb R)\) to \(r_x\) (the \(L^1\) predual is separable), and \(b_{n,x}\to\beta_{n,x}\) for each fixed coordinate. Put \(z_n=L\beta_n\). If \(z_{n,x}>0\), (3) and continuity of the Poisson kernels in \(L^1\) give \(\beta_{n,x}=f_x(z_{n,x})\), where \(f_x\) is the Poisson average of \(r_x\). In particular, for positive width this weight is positive exactly when \(r_x\) is not zero a.e. At zero width no conclusion about the limit of the Poisson average is assumed.

Call a channel thick if \(r_x\) is not zero a.e. Use the graph of positive \(L_{xy}\) on the thick vertices, counting a positive diagonal as a loop. If a nonthick vertex \(x\) has a thick neighbor \(y\), it cannot have \(\beta_{n,x}>0\) at any \(n\): this implies \(z_{n,y}>0\), then \(\beta_{n,y}>0\), then \(z_{n,x}>0\) and \(\beta_{n,x}=0\). Likewise both endpoints of any edge with simultaneous positive weights must be thick. In a thick component with at least one edge, positivity at one vertex propagates to all vertices at that frequency. Such a component at a given frequency is therefore either all zero or a positive solution \(z=L_D f(z)\), where \(D\) is the vertex set of the component; no nonthick neighbor contributes. A positive solution is unique: for positive arguments, every nonzero DOS gives \(|s f'_x(s)|<f_x(s)\) by the strict pointwise derivative inequality off a null set. If two solutions differ by a maximal multiplicative ratio \(r>1\) (consider both directions), all their Poisson averages differ by ratios strictly below \(r\), and the positive normal map then yields the same strict inequality for their widths, a contradiction.

A component positive at one frequency is positive at *every* fixed frequency on this subsequence. Suppose \(z^*>0\) solves the positive profile on \(D\), but \(z_{n,x}^\epsilon\to0\) there at another fixed \(n\). For \(h_x=f_x(z_x^*)/z_x^*\), the identity \(f_x(s)/s=\pi^{-1}\int r_x(u)/(s^2+u^2)\,du\) is strictly decreasing. Set \(a_x=z_x^*/2>0\); weak-* convergence at \(a_x\), this strict inequality, and monotonicity for each \(\epsilon\) show that eventually on all of \(D\),
\[
 b_{n,x}/z_{n,x}^\epsilon\ge f_x^\epsilon(a_x)/a_x\ge(1+\delta)h_x
 \tag{5}
\]
for some \(\delta>0\). But (4), with the fixed finite number of edges, then gives \(z_n^\epsilon\ge(1+\delta/2)L_D\operatorname{diag}(h)z_n^\epsilon\) on \(D\). Since \(L_D\operatorname{diag}(h)z^*=z^*\), take the minimum positive ratio of \(z_{n,x}^\epsilon/z_x^*\). Its minimizing row contradicts that inequality. This also covers a component consisting of a single thick vertex with a loop. Thus each positive component has one common frequency-independent profile \(\beta_x>0\). All other vertices are residual; every residual edge has \(J_{xy}\beta_{n,x}\beta_{n,y}=0\) at each same frequency. Edges between a positive component and its residual neighbors carry no product \(J_{xy}\beta_{n,x}\beta_{m,y}\) even across different frequencies: such a neighbor is nonthick and its weights vanish at all frequencies.

## 3. The stability limit and the collapsed sites

For finite \(\epsilon\) the normal forms are bounded on the \(g\) Hilbert space (use the uniform frequency rows and the bare widths), and finite-support tests are sufficient. Along a subsequence that approaches the liminf, take unit near-minimizing finite-support \(g^\epsilon\) and extract \(g^\epsilon\rightharpoonup g\) in the fixed angular-frequency space, filling absent coordinates with zero. The Hankel operators \(\mathscr H^\epsilon\) converge in Hilbert–Schmidt norm to \(\mathscr H\) obtained by substituting \(J/(n+m+1)^2\) and \(\beta\): the squared entries are dominated by the summable \(J_{xy}^2/[w_nw_m(n+m+1)^4]\), with fixed finite angular measure. The Hankel quadratic terms converge by compactness. Also \(g_{n,x}=0\) wherever \(\beta_{n,x}=0\) is **not** needed: such coordinates disappear from the Hankel limit, and their norm can be counted as nonnegative free bare cost. The near-minimizers can be chosen at bounded form value: if the relevant liminf is not finite above, (2) is already true. Therefore the difference squares in (1) are bounded in total.

On each positive component and at each fixed frequency, the same-frequency \(P^\epsilon\) diverges like \(L/\epsilon\) along every edge. With positive \(\beta\) at its ends, bounded cost forces \(v_{n,x}^\epsilon-v_{n,y}^\epsilon\to0\); hence the limit \(v_{n,x}=g_{n,x}/\sqrt{w_n\beta_x}\) is angularly constant there. Fatou's lemma gives a lower bound by the off-frequency limiting difference squares for coordinates with positive limiting weights. Squares with a missing endpoint can be dropped. The full result is at least \(1-\|g\|^2\) plus the limiting forms on the positive components and the residual part. Coordinates of \(g\) with zero limiting weight retain their nonnegative bare cost. There is no negative limiting cross term between these parts.

On a positive component \(D\) define \(B=\sum_{x\in D}\eta_x\beta_x\) and
\[
 j_D=\frac{\sum_{x,y\in D}\eta_x\eta_y J_{xy}\beta_x\beta_y}{B}.
\]
The angularly constant \(v_n\) has unit-frequency coordinate \(u_n=\sqrt{B w_n}\,v_n\). With the retained squares and the Hankel term, the limiting form is exactly \(\|u\|^2-j_D\langle u,\mathcal A u\rangle\), where \(\mathcal A\) is the scalar flat soft operator with \(\sup\sigma\mathcal A=\kappa\). This follows by expanding the difference squares or telescoping at each frequency; the same-frequency squares vanish. For reference, \(\mathcal A_{nm}=(|n-m|^{-2}+(n+m+1)^{-2})/\sqrt{w_nw_m}\) if \(n\ne m\), and \(\mathcal A_{nn}=(w_n^{-2}-2\sum_{j=1}^n j^{-2})/w_n\). The norm of the angular operator gives \(0\le j_D\le c\sum_{x\in D}\eta_x\beta_x^2/B\le c\). Thus the positive component costs at least \((1-\kappa c)\|u\|^2\).

On the residual vertices the limiting head-head contribution vanishes, since \(J_{xy}\beta_{0,x}\beta_{0,y}=0\). For head against tail, keep the positive cross entry \(S_{0m}\) when expanding the difference squares, dropping its two nonnegative diagonal terms. Its normalized frequency column is bounded by \(((m^{-2}+(m+1)^{-2})/\sqrt{w_m})_{m\ge1}\), with squared norm at most \(59/96<(4/5)^2\) as in [SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md](../SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md) §2. For tail against tail drop the nonnegative difference squares, leaving \(2H_{nm}/\sqrt{w_nw_m}\) of norm less than \(1/6\) for \(n,m\ge1\), by the same Schur test as there. The angular norm bound follows by taking absolute values at each frequency pair, applying \(\|J\|=c\) to angular norms, then applying the scalar frequency bounds; all multipliers \(\sqrt\beta\) are at most one. With \(a=\|g_0\|\), \(b=\|g_{\rm tail}\|\) restricted to residual vertices, the gain is at most \((8/5)c ab+(1/6)c b^2\le(29/30)c(a^2+b^2)\). This also bounds the free coordinates with zero limiting weights. Combining these bounds with \(1-\|g\|^2\) proves (2), since \(\kappa\ge1>29/30\). This treatment of the residual sites is needed: a vanishing scaled DOS is not, on its own, convergence to zero of every Cooper weight.

## 4. Exact limiting coefficient for scaled centered boxes

Suppose specifically \(r_x^\epsilon(u)=\mathbf1_{|u|<D_x/\epsilon}\) for fixed positive finite \(D_x\). The width bound from (3) at fixed frequencies yields positive lower bounds on all \(b_n\); all components with edges are positive and the unique profile solves \(\beta_x=(2/\pi)\arctan(D_x/(L\beta)_x)\). Isolated vertices have no pairing. The liminf argument gives \(\liminf a_\epsilon\ge 1-\kappa\max_D j_D\), since there are no interacting residual vertices. For the reverse inequality take finite frequency support with \(v_n\) constant on a chosen component, and zero elsewhere. Its same-frequency angular difference squares vanish identically already at finite \(\epsilon\). The other terms converge by dominated convergence (the normal rows between this support and its complement are summable; both endpoints outside the support contribute zero). Approximate the scalar top by finite support to get equality. If there are no interacting components, the value is 1. With finite \(D_x\) and a positive row, \(\beta_x<1\), so \(j_D<c\) for every coupled component if \(c>0\). This gives a positive margin at the nominal ceiling for that fixed normalized box family, not a uniform margin over arbitrary varying boxes. A single channel with \(D\uparrow\infty\) gives \(\beta\uparrow1\), and then \(j_D\uparrow c\). This proves sharpness of (2) for the stated limit.

For an explicit heterogeneous example, take \(\eta=(2,3)\), \(J=L\) with just the two reciprocal cross entries equal to one, and \((D_x)=(1,1/\sqrt3)\). Then \(L\beta=(1,1)\) at \(\beta=(1/2,1/3)\); the box formula gives that profile exactly. Here \(j_D=1\), while \(c=\sqrt6\). The two channels have unequal Poisson weights even though the limiting pairing trial is constant on both in the \(v\) coordinates.

## 5. Scope and verification

Sections 1–4 permit arbitrary centered DOS profiles and loss of all scaled DOS mass on some sites. They do not address noncentered frequency-dependent real normal shifts, simultaneous degeneration of the angular graph or the inverse-moment scaling, or a strict comparison at finite boson energy for general energy-dependent DOS. Section 6 treats flat DOS and sufficiently broad centered boxes at finite energy. None of these results constructs a matched counterexample. [Replay](replay.py) and [independent check](independent.py) verify finite identities and constants only; the graph selection, compactness and infinite-form arguments are analytic. No material or measured temperatures are used.

## 6. Arbitrary angular spectra at flat DOS (and sufficiently broad boxes)

**PROVED.** Let the finite angular system have a fixed physical reciprocal glue of finite moment and zero-transfer kernels, and use the same retarded kernel in the pairing and the normal equation. Let each DOS be constant in energy relative to its prescribed envelope: \(r_x(u)=\beta_x\) with \(0<\beta_x\le1\), omitting zero channels. Use the usual symmetric definition of the flat-band real normal integral, or the even centered branch directly. Neither the angular shapes of the spectra nor the resulting normal widths need be proportional. Then at \(\tau\ge C_\infty\sqrt{\rho}\) the form is strictly positive, with a gap also on an interval below the nominal ceiling when \(\rho>0\). Moreover, for fixed nonzero physical spectra, any family of centered finite boxes \(r_x(\xi)=\beta_x\mathbf1_{\{|\xi|<W_x\}}\) has this strict ceiling for all sufficiently large \(\min_xW_x\). Here the moment norm \(\rho\) is measured with the fixed envelope, i.e. on \(L^2(\eta)\).

At a fixed \(\tau\) let \(d\mu_{xy}\) be the physical moment divided by \((2\pi\tau)^2\) and \(t=E^2/(2\pi\tau)^2>0\) the spectral variable. For a unit moment at \(t\), use \(k_j=1/(j^2+t)\), \(B_{nm}=(k_{|n-m|}+k_{n+m+1})/\sqrt{w_nw_m}\), and \(d_n=(k_0+2\sum_{j=1}^n k_j)/w_n\). Define \(A_t=B-\operatorname{diag}d_n\). Its scalar spectral top is strictly less than \(\kappa\) by the imported scalar transfer comparison in the companion manuscript, Eq. (scalar comparison, `eq:scalar` in the source): for positive nonincreasing summable transfers at nonzero \(j\), increasing the transfers increases the spectral top, strictly if the first transfer increases. It applies because \(k_j\le 1/j^2\) for \(j\ge1\) and \(k_1<1\). No finite-band or anisotropic extension of that comparison is assumed.

We need an absolute norm bound, not just a bound on the positive top, since reciprocal angular matrices with nonnegative entries need not be positive semidefinite. We first show \(A_t\succeq-(13/10)I\) for every \(t>0\). On the full integer line the Fourier symbol of \(k_{|n-m|}\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\) is
\[
 F_t(\theta)=\frac{\pi\cosh(\sqrt t(\pi-|\theta|))}{\sqrt t\sinh(\pi\sqrt t)}.
\]
Integrating against \(e^{-ij\theta}/(2\pi)\) gives \(k_j\) (two integrations by parts on \(0<\theta<\pi\)); the absolute summability of the coefficients gives the equality on the whole interval. The symbol is minimized at \(\pi\), and \(F_t(\pi)\ge k_0-2(k_1-k_2+k_3)\) by the alternating series. Folding into the even subspace under \(n\mapsto -1-n\) is an isometric compression (use \(2^{-1/2}\) for each copy). After conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}w_n^{-1/2}\), subtraction of the normal diagonal therefore gives
\[
 A_t\succeq -\operatorname{diag}_n\frac{2(k_1-k_2+k_3)+2\sum_{j=1}^n k_j}{w_n}.
\]
The expression \(2(k_1-k_2+k_3)\) decreases with \(t\), since its derivative is \(-2(k_1^2-k_2^2+k_3^2)<0\). For \(t\ge1/3\) it is at most \(114/91\); rows \(0,1\) are bounded respectively by \(114/91\) and \((114/91+3/2)/3\), both below \(13/10\). At all \(t>0\) the restriction \(n\ge2\) is bounded below by \(-38/45\): at \(n=2\) use \(2(k_1-k_2+k_3)\le31/18\) and \(2(k_1+k_2)\le5/2\); at \(n\ge3\) use \(2\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}\le7/2\) and \(w_n\ge7\).

For \(0<t\le1/3\), the \(0,1\) principal block is at least \(-I\), because its diagonals are at least \(3/4,-2/3\), respectively, and its off-diagonal squared is at most \(25/48<(7/4)(1/3)\). Its cross block against \(m\ge2\) has squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm less than \(1/8\). Bound the terms \(2\le m\le4\) by their \(t=0\) off-diagonal limits; for \(m\ge5\) the sum of the two squares is at most \(8/[3(m-1)^5]\). The total is thus bounded by
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^1\sum_{m=2}^4\frac{((m-n)^{-2}+(m+n+1)^{-2})^2}{w_nw_m}
 +\frac{2}{3\cdot3^4}=\frac{58670711}{489888000}<\frac18 .
\]
The head and tail margins for \(A_t+(13/10)I\) have product \((3/10)(13/10-38/45)>1/8\). Cauchy–Schwarz across the two blocks proves the lower bound. Finally the \(t=0\) soft scalar trial \(u_n=\sqrt{w_n}\,(1,1/5,1/16)_n\), zero elsewhere, satisfies \(\langle u,(\mathcal A-(13/10)I)u\rangle=247/48000>0\). Thus \(13/10<\kappa\) and \(\|A_t\|<\kappa\) for every \(t>0\).

For flat DOS \(b_{n,x}=\beta_x\) identically on the actual normal solution. Use \(g_{n,x}=\sqrt{w_n\beta_x}v_{n,x}\) as before and write \(q_x=g_x/\sqrt{\beta_x}\) for the whole frequency vector. Expanding the original form with the exact normal rows gives
\[
 Q(g)=\|g\|^2-\sum_{x,y}\eta_x\eta_y\beta_x\beta_y
     \int\langle q_x,A_t q_y\rangle\,d\mu_{xy}(t)
 +\frac12\sum_{x,y}\eta_x\eta_y\beta_x\beta_y
     \int\sum_n d_n|q_{n,x}-q_{n,y}|^2\,d\mu_{xy}(t).
\]
Take the real part of the ordered cross expression if using complex vectors. The last term is the positive same-frequency angular normal cost that remains after splitting off \(A_t\). This uses the *normal row*, \(k_0+2\sum_{j=1}^nk_j\), not the diagonal \(k_0-k_{2n+1}\) of the normal kernel. With \(D_{xy}=\sqrt{\beta_x\beta_y}\int\|A_t\|d\mu_{xy}(t)\), the absolute value of the mixed term is at most \(\sum_{x,y}\eta_x\eta_y D_{xy}\|g_x\|\|g_y\|\le\|D\|\|g\|^2\). For finite channels and \(J\ne0\) there is a single \(\alpha<\kappa\) with \(D\le \alpha J\) entrywise, since the integral is strictly less than \(\kappa J_{xy}\) on each positive edge, and there are finitely many edges. Nonnegative entries imply \(\|D\|\le\alpha\|J\|\) by testing the absolute values of angular vectors. Hence the form gap is strictly positive at and above the nominal ceiling. For a fixed spectrum the signed operator is norm-continuous in positive \(\tau\) by bounded zero-transfer rows and summable nonzero-transfer domination, so the gap extends below the nominal ceiling. The optimal uniform flat coefficient follows from the scalar soft limit.

For finite boxes, fix the physical spectrum. At the nominal ceiling and on a compact temperature interval around it and up to the nonsharp ceiling, the exact centered equation has widths \(w_n\le s_{n,x}\le w_n+R\) with a single finite \(R\) (maximum angular row using the lower interval endpoint for \(j\ge1\)). Thus at every fixed \(n,x\), the Poisson factors converge to \(\beta_x\) uniformly in that interval as \(\min W_x\to\infty\): the argument of the arctangent is \(W_x/(\pi\tau s_{n,x})\). The pairing operators converge in norm uniformly by Hilbert–Schmidt domination. Indeed \(\sum_n(w_nw_{n+j})^{-1}\le\sum_n w_n^{-2}\) and the dominating transfer sequences are square summable, also covering the reflection since \(k_{n+m+1}\le k_{|n-m|}\). Normal entries are diagonal, bounded by \(R/w_n\), and converge entrywise on any finite frequency block uniformly by domination of each normal row. Hence the full signed form converges uniformly in operator norm. The flat gap has a positive minimum on a compact interval starting slightly below the nominal ceiling and extending through the nonsharp ceiling. Sufficiently broad boxes retain it there. Above that interval the general strict ceiling \(1/(\pi\sqrt2)\) from [SHARP-Q2](../SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md) already excludes onset. Neither this box argument nor the flat decomposition supplies a bandwidth bound uniform over arbitrary physical spectra.
