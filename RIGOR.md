# Passive retarded interactions and linear Eliashberg ceilings

All frequencies (bosonic transfer \(\nu\), boson energy \(E\), and \(\tau=k_BT\)) are in energy units. An instability means a negative value of the linearized normal-branch stability form; \(\tau_{\rm lin}\) is the supremum of such temperatures, zero for an empty unstable set. No assertion about a thermodynamic, measured, or exact Hamiltonian transition is made. The notation \(C_\infty\) denotes the exact spectral constant of case (e), not its roundoff.

Five proofs extend the original arguments below: [SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md](SHARP-Q2/RIGOR.md) upgrades the general norm coefficient to \(1/(\pi\sqrt2)\) under the stated form hypotheses, [PLASMON-W/RIGOR.md](PLASMON-W/RIGOR.md) proves the projected obstruction at fixed box bandwidth, [PHYS-Q2/RIGOR.md](PHYS-Q2/RIGOR.md) gives the sharp coefficient on actual matched finite-channel branches at flat DOS, in sufficiently broad centered boxes, and asymptotically under common soft scaling, [CENTERED-GAP/RIGOR.md](CENTERED-GAP/RIGOR.md) gives the strict, non-sharp upper coefficient \(U=\sqrt{6466219/3576050}/(2\pi)\) on finite centered branches at arbitrary glue energies, and [GENERAL/RIGOR.md](GENERAL/RIGOR.md) proves the same upper coefficient on causal shifted branches through the complementary-DOS Harnack estimate, as well as sharpness of \(1/(\pi\sqrt2)\) for unrestricted external shifts and the witness's failure of Schwarz-Pick. All five are in [the note](main.tex). The coupled relative-real optimum without an established causal continuation, and any shifted-versus-centered excess, remain OPEN. The older estimates below retain their original scope.

## 1. PROVED: passive-screening representation and sharp matched scalar result

Assume a real reciprocal scalar transfer (or a specified reciprocal pair of scalar channels) with retarded analytic continuation in the upper half-plane, reality \(W^R(-\bar z)=\overline{W^R(z)}\), and a real finite large-frequency term \(U\). Require the **unsubtracted** dispersion relation for \(W^R-U\), including any atoms of its boundary loss. One sufficient hypothesis is a vanishing large-semicircle contour integral for this difference in Cauchy's formula. Write \(dB(E)=-\pi^{-1}\Im W^R(E+i0)\,dE\) on \(E>0\), in the measure sense, and require \(dB\ge0\). A genuine zero-frequency singular term is not included. The oddness of the loss pairs the two sides of the real axis:
\[
 W(i\nu)-U=\int_0^\infty\left(\frac1{i\nu-E}-\frac1{i\nu+E}\right)dB(E)
 =-\int_0^\infty\frac{2E}{E^2+\nu^2}\,dB(E).
\]
Hence the **pairing attraction** \(-W\) is \(-U+K\) with \(K_j=K(2\pi\tau j)\), \(d\mathfrak m=2E\,dB(E)\), \(K(0)=\int E^{-2}d\mathfrak m\) and \(J=\int d\mathfrak m=\lim_{|\nu|\to\infty}\nu^2 K(\nu)\) by monotone convergence, including an infinite limit. A constant cannot change this moment, since \(U=\lim_{|\nu|\to\infty}W(i\nu)\) when \(K(0)<\infty\). In a microscopic normalization, \(W\) has units of energy times normalization volume, \(J\) of energy cubed times volume, and a DOS per unit volume per unit energy makes its product with \(J\) an energy squared. No factor of two for spin is included in the pairing DOS convention. A phonon with transfer \(2|g|^2E/(E^2+\nu^2)\) adds \(2|g|^2E\) to \(J\) in compatible normalization. Independent positive measures add, not their square roots.

PSD of \(U\) **in the pairing form** is a separate assumption. Passivity constrains only loss, not the large-frequency term. Operator passivity yields PSD measures in the **response space**, by testing its dispersion relation on all vectors and polarizing. This does not by itself give pointwise nonnegative scalar measures for reciprocal pairing-channel transfers. For instance \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) is PSD with negative off-diagonal entries; a signed angular projection can also arise even when every transfer is positive before projection (§5). In a translation-invariant density channel with a scalar \(W(q,z)\), pointwise nonnegative loss is the appropriate hypothesis on each \(q\). Bare Coulomb is PSD in the unprojected relative-coordinate pairing form by Fourier transformation to multiplication by \(e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|r|)\); a subsequent specified orthogonal projection preserves PSD. Do not substitute scalar positivity of matrix elements for PSD of a pairing form.

**Matched theorem.** Let the interaction be radially independent within a scalar channel, \(0<N_{\max}=\operatorname{ess\,sup}N<\infty\), and require \(0<K(0),J<\infty\) for the **total** attractive transfer. Use the same DOS and retarded transfer in the anomalous and normal equations, without vertex terms. Assume a positive reflected normal branch obeying both the odd normal mass and the real equation relative to the zeroth Matsubara mode, with defined relative sums in ordinary Matsubara order. The imported result is “Self-consistent DOS, computer-assisted” in “Frequency-dependent centers and repulsion” of Rens's finite-band ceiling preprint (2026), public Zenodo version 1.0.0 [10.5281/zenodo.24364879](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24364879), with [supplement](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24364881) and [repository](https://github.com/SuperMatics-Inc/eliashberg-finiteband-ceiling). The theorem there takes no static pairing term. Here an instantaneous subtraction may be any PSD form defined on the pairing domain: \(L^*UL\) is sufficient for arbitrary admissible vertex map \(L\). A static normal term can be different and can be absorbed into the dispersion before defining the DOS height.

Set \(d\lambda=N_{\rm ref}E^{-2}d\mathfrak m\), \(M=N_{\rm ref}J\), \(A=N_{\max}/N_{\rm ref}\). The retarded equations are exactly case (e); it assumes a positive measure with these finite moments, not that its origin is a phonon. On the **actual** normal branch the static term subtracts a positive form in the symmetrized gap operator. The Rayleigh comparison with the matched attractive problem on that same branch gives
\[
 \tau_{\rm lin}< C_\infty\sqrt{N_{\max}J}=C_\infty\sqrt{AM}.
\]
Case (e) gives the common stability interval extending strictly below the nominal ceiling at fixed spectrum, without branch continuity. It also supplies the sharpness sequence of finite bands and soft Einstein spectra; \(U=0\) is allowed in the class. Strictness is not a claim of sharpness at given nonzero \(U\). If \(J=0\) and no other pairing term is present beyond PSD repulsion, there is no instability. The balanced common-DOS reduction in G3COMBINE Q2 requires \(0<\eta(X)<\infty\), balance of each transfer row, and a common actual branch. Under these hypotheses it extends this sharp result. For DOS constant on the entire energy line, GSI Theorems 7–9 supply the sharp flat theorem (strict for finitely many angular channels, nonstrict in the continuum). Static PSD comparison works in either model. Neither identification proves the sharp coefficient for general coupled branches with distinct DOS profiles.

**A heterogeneous-DOS exact reduction.** There is no intersector retarded transfer, and within each sector the matched normal and anomalous retarded transfer is independent of both angles and both radial energies. For one such sector \(X\), write \(d\mu\) for its bare channel measure, \(N_x(\xi)\) for each DOS after any frequency-independent offset is absorbed into \(\xi\), and \(N_\Sigma(\xi)=\int_X N_x(\xi)d\mu(x)\). Assume \(\int_X N_{\max}(x)d\mu(x)<\infty\) and the scalar branch and relative convergence conditions. The common kernel gives identical dynamic normal widths and relative real shifts for all \(x\); linearity under the normal energy integral gives exactly the scalar equations with \(N_\Sigma\). The retarded pairing interaction vanishes on the orthogonal complement of the fiber vectors \(\sqrt{\tau/[u_n^2+(\xi+\chi_n)^2]}\) in \(L^2(N_x(\xi)d\xi\,d\mu)\); its compression to their span is the scalar pairing operator with \(N_\Sigma\). Static PSD cannot raise its top even if it couples sectors. Thus the sharp scale in a sector uses \(\operatorname{ess\,sup}N_\Sigma\), potentially smaller than \(\int N_{\max}d\mu\) when the DOS peaks are staggered.

## 2. PROVED: screened charge f-sum, its logarithm, and what can be made finite

Take \(W=v+v^2\chi^R\) with the **reducible** density response and the external density coupling normalized by volume \(V\). In a box let \(\rho_q=\sum_{i=1}^{N_e}e^{-iq\cdot r_i}\) and use the retarded-response convention in which its positive-energy loss is nonnegative. The Lehmann formula gives
\[
 2\int_0^\infty E\,dB_\chi(q,E)=V^{-1}\langle[\rho_{-q},[H,\rho_q]]\rangle.
\]
To check the normalization, in an energy basis the right side sums \((p_a-p_b)(E_b-E_a)|\langle b|\rho_q|a\rangle|^2/V\) over all ordered pairs; interchanging \(a,b\) in the negative-energy half gives the same positive-energy weight at \(-q\). Equality as written assumes equivalent \(q,-q\) loss (or uses their average). This uses total density, including spin. For \(p_i^2/(2m)\), the two kinetic shifts by \(\pm\hbar q\) sum to \(\hbar^2|q|^2/m\); coordinate-dependent interactions commute with the density. Thus for continuum jellium, at \(q\ne0\),
\[
 J(q)=v(q)^2 \frac{n_e\hbar^2q^2}{m} = \frac{J_C}{q^2},\qquad
 J_C=\frac{n_e\hbar^2}{m}\left(\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0}\right)^2
\]
using SI \(v(q)=e^2/(\epsilon_0 q^2)\) and Fourier measure \(d^3q/(2\pi)^3\). Equivalently \(J(q)=v(q) E_p^2\), \(E_p^2=\hbar^2n_e e^2/(m\epsilon_0)\). No RPA assumption or concentration of the loss at \(E_p\) enters the f-sum. The background removes the point \(q=0\), not a neighborhood of that point.

In the spherical on-shell projection \(q=2k_F\sin(\theta/2)\) the normalized angular row measure is \(q\,dq/(2k_F^2)\). Removing \(q<\delta\), the moment row and operator norm are both \(J_C\log(2k_F/\delta)/(2k_F^2)\) (constant row, nonnegative kernel, constant-vector eigenfunction). A positive DOS normalization multiplies this result. Any continuous angular form factor nonzero at the forward point retains the local log there; for a fixed spherical harmonic, \(P_\ell(\cos\theta)=1+O(q^2)\), the divergent part of the eigenvalue is identical. An imposed coupling form factor vanishing as \(O(q^a)\), \(a>0\), in the **moment kernel** makes the forward integral finite, but changes the interaction. The Fermi-surface-only projection diverges while a bounded full three-dimensional momentum shell has no infrared divergence: its row is bounded by \(J_C\int_{|q|\le2R}d^3q/[(2\pi)^3q^2]=J_C R/\pi^2\) when all electronic momenta lie in the radius-\(R\) ball. Without the electronic shell or a weight, the whole-space row also diverges in the UV. The unweighted \(L^2\) moment operator is unbounded: normalized positive constant tests on balls of radius \(R\) give Rayleigh quotients growing by an exact factor \(R\) under scaling.

A constant subtraction or reassignment between instantaneous and retarded terms preserving \(W\) cannot remove its positive f-sum coefficient. A transfer subtraction \(K(\nu)-K(\nu_0)\) generally changes the high-frequency constant; if this constant is reassigned into \(U\) the moment stays the same. Moreover the f-sum does not imply any angular cancellation of distinct transfer kernels. For example the passive neutralized single-plasmon model \(W(q,i\nu)=v(q)\nu^2/(E_p^2+\nu^2)\), consistent with that same f-sum, diverges angularly at each nonzero \(\nu\); two distinct absolute transfers have different leading coefficients. Subtracting the full forward plasmon term could change that specific model into a finite kernel, but is a change of pairing and mass, not an equivalent PSD subtraction or a universal bound for the original equation. If high-energy spectral tails instead make the moment infinite but keep each transfer finite with bounded rows, §3 gives a finite-temperature test using the actual Hankel transfer, without replacing it by a divergent moment. Neither this test nor the f-sum proves that an unregularized on-shell jellium pairing operator is defined or that its transition scale is infinite.

**The unchanged reduced equation has no uniform finite cutoff bound, even with static repulsion.** This is a model statement, not a jellium prediction. In the single-plasmon model restrict to the spherical constant harmonic and remove \(q<\delta\). The scalar retarded transfers are \(C_\delta g_j\), \(g_j=E_p^2/[E_p^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2]\), with instantaneous \(C_\delta>0\) proportional to \(\log(2k_F/\delta)\). Take flat DOS and initially work on finite-support even tests \(v\). The total instantaneous cost is a positive multiple of \(|\sum_n v_n|^2\). After normal cancellation the diagonal retarded gain, per \(C_\delta\), is \(g_{2n+1}-2\sum_{j=1}^n g_j\): this follows by telescoping the folded odd normal row \(g_0+2\sum_{j=1}^n g_j\). Its head value is \(g_1>0\) and its lower bound is \(-2L\), where \(L=\sum_{j\ge1}g_j<\infty\). Off-diagonal gain is \(g_{|n-m|}+g_{n+m+1}\). For \(v_0=1,\ v_m=-1/N\) on \(N\le m<2N\), zero elsewhere, the static cost vanishes, the tail off-diagonal gain is nonnegative, the tail diagonal gain is at least \(-2L/N\), and the head-tail gain is at least \(-4L/N\). Choose an integer \(N>6L/g_1\). The bare cost \(\sum_n w_n|v_n|^2\) is finite, so the divergent \(C_\delta\) defeats it at every prescribed test temperature. At any fixed \(\delta\) and test temperature the same strict instability persists for sufficiently wide finite centered box bands: \(b_m\to1\) pointwise by the width row bound, all folded sums converge by domination, and replace the nonzero \(v_m\) by \(v_m/b_m\) to keep the static cost zero. Hence this is also a family of **finite-band** cutoff models with no uniform finite bound as both cutoff and bandwidth vary. It does not exclude finite bounds at a *fixed* bandwidth, or for actual full-momentum jellium.

For a one-band lattice Hamiltonian with density-dependent interactions and band \(\epsilon(k)\), the same double commutator equals \(V^{-1}\sum_{k,\sigma}\langle n_{k\sigma}\rangle[\epsilon(k+q)+\epsilon(k-q)-2\epsilon(k)]\). It is bounded by \(4\|\epsilon\|_\infty n_e\); for bounded band Hessian its small-\(q\) magnitude is at most \(n_e\|D^2\epsilon\|_\infty |q|^2\), by two Taylor integrals. For bounded Fourier \(v\) (e.g. finite-range lattice density interaction), compact Brillouin zone gives a bounded moment kernel and finite moment operator on finite channel measure. A finite \(K(0)\) is a separate low-energy condition. A lattice with unscreened **bare** long-range Coulomb and nonzero quadratic coefficient need not remove the forward log on a regular 3D Fermi surface. In the continuum, integrable finite-range \(v(r)\) ensures bounded \(v(q)\), hence no forward divergence on bounded momentum support. It does not by itself bound the unrestricted 3D moment row in the UV: its radial majorant involves \(\int q^4 |v(q)|^2dq\) (or the corresponding nonradial integral), finite, for example, with \(\nabla v\in L^2\). These statements concern the density f-sum, not spin, orbital, or vertex-weighted responses with a different commutator.

## 3. PROVED: anisotropic height and true-momentum bounds

Let \(X\) be the reciprocal channel space with \(d\eta(x)=N_{\max}(x)d\mu(x)\) (omit zero-height channels). Bare kernels here are independent of both radial energies. Set \(w_n=2n+1\), \(s_n(x)=u_n(x)/(\pi\tau)>0\), and \(b_n(x)\in[0,1]\) equal to the Poisson average of the normalized channel DOS at the actual shift and width. Let \(K_j\) denote reciprocal nonnegative Lorentzian mixtures (the matched glue or a pairing envelope). Define \(D_{nm}=K_{|n-m|}\), \(H_{nm}=K_{n+m+1}\), \(P=D-H\ge0\) entrywise. Require bounded row sums of \(K_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}K_j\) for the branch/operator formulation here, and a bounded moment operator \(\mathcal M\) on \(L^2(d\eta)\) with kernel \(\mathfrak m_{xy}((0,\infty))\) and norm \(\rho\). By nonnegative entries its spectral top equals its norm. For an unmatched normal transfer the odd mass equation must be \(s_n=w_n+\sum_m\int P^N_{nm}(x,y)b_m(y)d\eta(y)\), with \(P^N\ge P\) entrywise and bounded normal rows. A real equation is not needed for this nonsharp comparison. If the actual full-line retarded pairing kernel is signed, require its modulus transfer by transfer to be bounded by \(K_j\); a PSD instantaneous term may be subtracted afterwards. Taking moduli on the full line and then the root mean square of the two reflected amplitudes at each channel and index bounds the signed full-line spectral top by the even comparator. Indeed each direct and reflected nonnegative bilinear term is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz at that pair of channels and positive indices. No even-momentum or even-frequency restriction on the original trial is needed.

In even coordinates \(f_n(x)=\sqrt{b_ns_n}\,v_n(x)\), omit zero-weight fibers. The matched comparator form is
\[
 Q=\sum_n\int w_n b_n|v_n|^2 +\tfrac12\sum_{n,m}\iint P_{nm}b_nb_m'|v_n-v_m'|^2
      -2\sum_{n,m}\iint H_{nm}b_nb_m'\Re(\bar v_nv_m').
\]
Here single and double integrals use \(d\eta\) and \(d\eta\,d\eta'\). For the actual normal branch add \(\sum_n\int b_n|v_n|^2\sum_m\int(P^N_{nm}-P_{nm})b'_m\ge0\). The identity follows by symmetry after expanding the square and using \(D+H=P+2H\), first on supported trials and then by bounded form extension. In \(g_n=\sqrt{w_n b_n}\,v_n\), the last term is bounded in absolute value by \(2\rho\|\mathcal H\|\|g\|^2/(2\pi\tau)^2\), with \(\mathcal H_{nm}=(w_nw_m)^{-1/2}(n+m+1)^{-2}\), using \(b\le1\) and the angular norm on each block. The bare term is \(\|g\|^2\); \(\|g\|^2\ge\|f\|^2/(1+D_*)\) for a bound \(D_*\) on the normal rows. For a finite-transfer model without finite moment one can instead use \(2\|\mathcal H_K(\tau)\|<1\), with \((\mathcal H_K)_{nm}=K_{n+m+1}/\sqrt{w_nw_m}\) as angular operators. This is a sufficient criterion at the indicated temperature, not a uniform finite bound on all models sharing an infinite moment.

**Rational tail improvement.** \(\mathcal H_{00}=1\). The Schur row bound of its tail \(n,m\ge1\), with weights \(w_n^{-1/2}\), is \(\sum_{m\ge1}[(2m+1)(m+2)^2]^{-1}<1/12\); the squared norm of its cross vector is \(\sum_{m\ge1}[(2m+1)(m+1)^4]^{-1}<1/40\). Sum through 16 and bound the omitted tails by \(\int_{16}^\infty (2t^3)^{-1}dt\) and \(\int_{16}^\infty (2t^5)^{-1}dt\) respectively. At \(a=113/110=1+3/110\), \((a-1)(a-1/12)>1/40\), so block-square completion gives \(\|\mathcal H\|<113/110\) (nonnegative entries let its top control the norm). Consequently
\[
 \tau_{\rm lin}<\frac{\sqrt{113/55}}{2\pi}\sqrt\rho.
\]
This improves the prior Schur coefficient in G3COMBINE, but is not asserted optimal. The inherited sharp lower example and this upper bound bracket the possible general coefficient. A free choice of the numbers \(b_n(x)\) is not a self-consistent DOS counterexample. The needed control of signed normal terms by the coupled relative real/imaginary equations, or an actual self-consistent counterexample, is open.

The strict coefficient statement is for \(\rho>0\). For \(\rho=0\), positivity forces the retarded pairing envelope to vanish, and a PSD subtraction cannot give an instability.

**Sharper row bound, optimal for independent weights.** If \(R=\operatorname{ess\,sup}_x\int \mathfrak m_{xy}((0,\infty))d\eta(y)<\infty\), also write \(c=R/(2\pi\tau)^2\). In \(g\) coordinates split the form at \(n=0\). Its head alone is bounded below by \((1-2\|K_1\|)\|g_0\|^2+\int d_0|g_0|^2\), \(d_0(x)=\sum_{m\ge1}\int P_{0m}(x,y)b_m(y)d\eta(y)\): retain the diagonal normal cost from head-tail edges and discard the nonnegative \(P\) square within the head. Its tail alone is bounded below by \((1-c/6)\|g_{\rm tail}\|^2\), discarding all positive contributions from \(P\) and using the tail Hankel estimate above. If \(L^*g_0\) is the connecting block with kernel \((D+H)_{0m}\sqrt{b_0b_m/w_m}\), for each \(m\ge1,y\) Cauchy–Schwarz in \(x\) gives
\[
 |(L^*g_0)_m(y)|^2\le b_m(y)
 \left(\int\frac{(D+H)_{0m}^2}{w_m P_{0m}}d\eta(x)\right)
 \left(\int P_{0m} b_0|g_0|^2d\eta(x)\right).
\]
 The zero transfer measure gives zero in the quotient. The first integral is at most \(25c/36\): by Cauchy–Schwarz in the Lorentzian measure its integrand is at most \(\mathfrak m_{xy}((0,\infty))/(2\pi\tau)^2\) times \(\sup_{t\ge0}[4/w_m^2+1/((t+m^2)(t+(m+1)^2))]\le25/36\). Integrating and summing shows \(\|L^*g_0\|^2\le(25c/36)\int d_0|g_0|^2\). Completing the square against the tail costs no more than \((25c/36)/(1-c/6)\) times this retained normal term. For \(c\le1/2\) this is at most \(25/66<1\). The test therefore gives stability for \(c<1/2\), and a gap at \(c=1/2\) if \(\|K_1\|<c\) (in particular for finitely many positive-height channels). Thus
\[
 \tau_{\rm lin}\le \frac{\sqrt R}{\pi\sqrt2},
\]
 with strict inequality for fixed finitely many channels when \(R>0\). The two bounds can be minimized if both moments are known. The constant in this row bound cannot be decreased for the relaxed model of arbitrary independent \(b_n(x)\in[0,1]\): a scalar channel with \(b_0=1,b_{m>0}=0\) and a soft line approaches the asserted threshold. This is not a sharpness claim on self-consistent branches. Replacing the row \(R\) in the cross Cauchy–Schwarz estimate by \(\rho\) does not follow from the operator norm; the two moments can differ without bound.

**True momentum before projecting to the Fermi surface.** Use one radial/momentum point per coordinate, with measure \(d\kappa=d^3k/(2\pi)^3\), and no radial independence assumption. Assume the same reciprocal energy-transfer kernels and corresponding pointwise odd-folded normal dominance. Let \(e_n(k)\) be the real shifted dispersion and put \(b_n(k)=(\pi\tau)u_n/(u_n^2+e_n^2)\), \(0\le b_n\le1/w_n\). Now both normal and anomalous equations have the common extra prefactor \(1/(\pi^2\tau)\) in the \(s_n\) normalization: \(\tau u_m/(u_m^2+e_m^2)=b_m/\pi\), then divide widths by \(\pi\tau\). The preceding square identity is unchanged except for this prefactor on all interaction terms and the momentum measure. On a bounded domain of moment norm \(\rho_{\rm bulk}\), a sufficient stability criterion is
\[
 \tau^3>\frac{\rho_{\rm bulk}\|\mathcal H\|}{2\pi^4}.
\]
We used just \(b\le1\); \(b\le1/w_n\) would further tighten the frequency operator. Thus this finite sufficient scale is cubic, not the sharp projected square-root scale. All bounds are on the form domain, and hence on its closure when that closure defines the linearized model; normal existence is not proved by the bounds. Static repulsion is retained as PSD, not discarded at the equation level. For the Coulomb f-sum on the ball use the row bound of §2. \(\rho_{\rm bulk}\) here has units of energy cubed.

There is also an uncut conditional version. Suppose on the candidate uncut jellium branch \(|e_n(k)|\ge a|k|^2\) for \(|k|>R\), uniformly in \(n\), with \(a>0\), and the full normal and pairing forms are defined. Since \(u/(u^2+e^2)\le1/(2|e|)\), \(b_n\le F_L(k)=\min(1,L^2/|k|^2)\) where \(L=\max(R,\sqrt{\pi\tau/(2a)})\). Bound the Hankel term as before with \(\mathcal M\) replaced by \(F_L^{1/2}\mathcal M F_L^{1/2}\). Its norm is at most \(J_C L/\pi^2\): apply Schur with weight \(F_L^{1/2}\); the remaining convolution of two radial decreasing functions is maximized at zero (layer cake of two balls, whose intersection volume cannot exceed that for concentric balls). At zero it is \(J_C/(2\pi^2)\int_0^\infty F_L(r)dr=J_C L/\pi^2\). This gives the finite sufficient condition \(\tau^3>J_C L\|\mathcal H\|/(2\pi^6)\) on such branches. It is not a proof of the uniform dispersion coercivity, an existence result for the uncut Coulomb Eliashberg branch, or a statement about actual jellium. If coercivity holds with fixed constants at high temperature the sufficient condition is eventually satisfied.

## 4. PROVED: the useful normal-dominance hypotheses

On a **specified** branch, holding its energy measure and propagator fixed, the extra folded normal term in §3 is nonnegative. Spectral measure dominance of a nonnegative pairing envelope by the normal spectrum is sufficient: \((E^2+(2\pi\tau |n-m|)^2)^{-1}-(E^2+(2\pi\tau(n+m+1))^2)^{-1}\ge0\) before integration. This proves the §3 bound with the pairing-envelope moment, not the larger normal moment. For a signed measure an admissible envelope is total variation; a smaller specified positive envelope works if it majorizes the transfers and is itself folded dominated. For a channel obtained by symmetry reduction one must check the actual projected transfers and propagators. An angular block that is not invariant under the propagator cannot be replaced by an independent scalar channel.

For flat DOS the weights \(b=1\) are unchanged by extra normal scattering. The matched comparator on the same DOS has \(s^P=w+P b\le s^N\); on the full symmetrized gap operator the change is a congruence by the diagonal contraction \(\sqrt{s^P/s^N}\). A self-adjoint operator bounded above by \(\ell I\) with \(\ell\ge0\) stays bounded above by \(\ell I\) under this congruence; modulus comparison deals with the signed pair kernel first. This proves the sharp pairing-envelope ceiling in the scalar and flat anisotropic settings of §1 (with the strictness supplied by their matched theorem). In particular a scalar symmetry channel with a nonnegative projected spectrum and dominating normal spectrum is covered at flat DOS; its gap need not be s-wave. Similarly on finite bands, the sharp comparator applies if there is an actual matched case-(e) comparator on the same angular/energy measure with pointwise larger Cooper weights \(q^P_n=\tau/[(u^P_n)^2+(e_n^P)^2]\ge q^N_n\) and common pairing envelope. Energy-space multiplication by \(\sqrt{q^N/q^P}\) is again a diagonal contraction. This condition is sufficient, not deduced here from normal spectral dominance alone on self-consistent finite bands. Changing the underlying branch generally changes \(b\) and the real shifts; an increase in the normal kernel does not justify comparing onsets on different branches by the fixed-branch form identity.

**Counterexample to onset monotonicity on finite bands, with spectral dominance.** Keep the reciprocal pairing measures fixed in two centered channels \(A,B\), with \(d\eta(A)=1,\ d\eta(B)=10^{12}\), and DOS boxes of halfwidths 100 and \(10^7\), respectively. Use Einstein transfers at temperature \(\tau\)
\[
 K_{AA,j}=\frac4{1+(\tau j)^2},\qquad
 K_{AB,j}=K_{BA,j}=\frac{10^{-9}}{1+10^4(\tau j)^2},\qquad K_{BB,j}=0 .
\]
 The outgoing zero-transfer cross weights are \(10^3\) in the \(A\) normal row and \(10^{-9}\) in the \(B\) row. No static pairing term is needed. Use the centered normal equations, with \(b_i=(2/\pi)\arctan(W_i/(\pi\tau s_i))\) at each positive Matsubara index for the corresponding halfwidth \(W_i\). There is a unique centered width solution for either normal kernel at each \(\tau>0\): \(|d\log b_i/d\log s_i|\le1\) from the Poisson integral, and the logarithm of the positive normal map on \(w_n\le s_n\le w_n+D_*\) is a sup-metric contraction with factor at most \(D_* /(1+D_*)<1\). Here \(D_*\) is a uniform upper bound for the nonnegative normal row sum at that temperature. The real equations vanish by symmetry.

 First take the matched normal spectrum. For all \(\tau\ge1\), the full even scalar \(AA\) transfer has frequency row sum at most \(4+8\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}\le20\). On \(1\le\tau\le1000\), if \(w_n<40\), then \(s_{B,n}\le w_n+2\cdot10^{-9}<41\), so \(\pi\tau s_{B,n}<10^7\) and \(b_{B,n}>1/2\). The equal-index term of the cross normal row gives \(s_{A,n}\ge1000/4=250\) on these indices. For \(w_n\ge40\) use \(s_{A,n}\ge w_n\). Thus \(b_{A,n}/s_{A,n}\le1/40\) throughout that temperature interval, and the \(AA\) gap block has norm at most \(1/2\). For \(\tau\ge1000\) use instead \(b_{A,n}/s_{A,n}\le 200/(\pi^2\tau)\), giving norm less than \(4/9\). The symmetric angular cross block has norm at most \(\sqrt{10^3\,10^{-9}}(1+4/10^4)<1/100\), by its folded transfer row and \(b/s\le1\). The \(BB\) gap block is zero. Nonnegative comparison excludes other frequency parity sectors too. The baseline therefore has \(\tau_{\rm lin}\le1\).

 Now add only the normal \(BB\) line with weighted transfer \(10^{16}/(1+(\tau j)^2)\) and leave pairing unchanged. It dominates the pairing spectrum as a measure in every entry. At \(\tau=101/100\), the normal first \(B\) row has \(s_{B,0}\ge (10^{16}/2)b_{B,0}\). Since \(b_{B,0}\le 2\cdot10^7/(\pi^2\tau s_{B,0})\), this implies \(b_{B,0}^2\le4\cdot10^7/(9\cdot10^{16})<10^{-8}\). The first \(A\) normal row has \(s_{A,0}\le1+4+1000b_{B,0}+1000/(1+10^4\tau^2)<26/5\), using telescoping to bound the nonzero-index rows. Thus \(b_{A,0}\ge1-2\tau s_{A,0}/100>89/100\). The even \(A\)-only head trial in the same pairing operator has quotient
\[
 \frac{(K_{AA,0}+K_{AA,1})b_{A,0}}{s_{A,0}}
 >
 \frac{(59/10)(89/100)}{26/5}>1.
\]
 Hence the modified system has \(\tau_{\rm lin}\ge101/100\), strictly above the matched system. The physical mechanism *in this model* is negative feedback: broadening the partner channel removes its DOS weight from the active channel's normal mass, while the symmetric pairing cross block is already small. This disproves a monotonic-onset argument for the sharp pairing-moment bound under branch changes, not the sharp pairing-moment inequality itself. The pairing moment norm is unchanged, and this witness is below its putative \(C_\infty\) scale.

The added line is normal-only in this effective model. Adding a matched phonon would also change the pairing kernel. This example makes no claim about realistic couplings, equal total spectral weight of microscopic bands, or priority for scattering-induced onset enhancement.

## 5. PROVED necessity examples and limits on what they establish

1. **Passivity is not PSD of the instantaneous pairing subtraction.** In two equally weighted channels (counting measure), take constant \(U=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&3\\3&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) and zero loss. It is passive, reciprocal, pointwise repulsive, with eigenvalue \(-2\) in the antisymmetric channel. Flat DOS and \(s=w\) give even-frequency zeroth-mode pairing quotient \(4\) there at any fixed \(\tau\). To keep the instantaneous pairing operator ordinarily bounded on the full frequency line, instead take the same centered finite box \([-100,100]\) in both channels at \(\tau=1\) with static real shifts absorbed. Then \(b_0>49/50\) and the quotient exceeds \(98/25\); at high temperature the finite-mass pairing operator tends to zero. The positive spectral moment can be made arbitrarily small by adding identical weak retarded transfers in both diagonal channels; the finite-support strict margin persists by the centered normal contraction or row bounds. Thus PSD cannot be inferred from a positive pointwise static interaction or passivity.
2. **The signed projected moment is not the pairing budget.** For two equivalent patches with counting measure consider a positive diagonal transfer \(K^+_j=8/(1+j^2)\) and positive interpatch transfer \(K^-_j=8/(64+j^2)\) at \(\tau=1\). Each is an Einstein transfer with moment \(8(2\pi)^2\). The antisymmetric (sign-changing) angular sector pairs with \(K^P=K^+-K^-\). The common flat normal sector uses \(K^N=K^++K^-\), exactly the total-variation envelope, so its normal spectrum dominates. The signed moment vanishes; all \(K^P_j>0\) and are decreasing in \(j\), so neither positivity nor monotonicity of Matsubara values is enough. Formula \(s_0=1+K^N_0\) by telescoping gives the even zeroth-mode quotient \((K^P_0+K^P_1)/s_0=6111/4745>1\). A small decrease of the negative line makes the signed moment positive and arbitrarily small without losing the witness. For finite sufficiently wide identical centered boxes the normalized widths are bounded by the full row at fixed spectrum, \(b_m\to1\) pointwise as bandwidth tends to infinity, and summable \(P^N_{0m}\) allows dominated convergence in the witness. Standard finite-band high-temperature stability then ensures a finite onset scale with this violation. Angular antisymmetry is a symmetry representation in the patch model, not a microscopic claim about spin-fluctuation cuprates.
3. **Raw transfer dominance does not imply folded normal dominance on a finite band.** With one even box DOS of height 1 on \([-100,100]\), and at \(\tau=1\), use \(K^N_j=4R^2/(R^2+j^2)\), \(R=10^4\); let \(K^P_j=4x/(x+j^2)\) for \(0<x\le1\). Normal dominates at every \(\nu\) (even continuous transfer) and both spectra are positive, but the normal transfer has no spectral mass at the pairing line. The centered normal branch exists by the scalar centered construction with \(K^N\). With \(J_0=1000\), the first normal row obeys
   \[
   s_0=1+\sum_{m\ge0}(K^N_m-K^N_{m+1})b_m
     \le 1+\frac4{101}+\frac{800}{9(2001)}<\frac{11}{10}.
   \]
   The head uses \(b_m\le1\) and telescoping at \(m=J_0\); the tail uses \(b_m=(2/\pi)\arctan(100/(\pi s_m))\le200/(9w_m)\), then sums normal differences to at most 4. Hence \(b_0\ge1-2s_0/100>489/500\); the zeroth-mode quotient is at least \(K^P_0 b_0/s_0>978/275\). The height-weighted pairing moment is \(4x(2\pi)^2\to0\). At all temperatures the gap operator with finite DOS mass \(D\) has norm bounded by \(K^P_0 D/(4\tau)\), by the full-line diagonal propagator bound and \(\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(2n+1)^{-2}=\pi^2/4\), so this is a genuine instability at or below a finite high-temperature stability interval. The model tests raw dominance, not stronger spectral dominance. Omitting all normal mass gives an even simpler pairing-only variant of this failure.
4. **Counting only phonons, and using only on-shell data.** G3COMBINE Q3 already proves instability at \(\tau=1\) for the fixed causal screened transfer \(5-4E^2/(E^2+\nu^2)\) with \(E=2\pi\), included in normal and pairing, as the phonon moment tends to zero. The Lorentzian screening weight has a nonzero moment; this is not a refutation of §1. G3COMBINE Q2 proves instability with zero Fermi-surface vertex by moving the vertex off shell, and with arbitrarily small \(N(0)\) by filling a notch. Neither refutes the height-weighted theorem. Averages in angle are insufficient even at flat DOS: take two uncoupled flat patches with measures \(p,1-p\), active bare moment \(J/p\) on the first patch, zero elsewhere. The moment norm is \(J\) while the angular double average is \(pJ\); the sharp scalar active-block instability does not go to zero as \(p\downarrow0\). Reciprocity and the same DOS in normal and pairing are assumed for the symmetric form; a general nonreciprocal kernel lacks that variational interpretation. No claim that every violation of reciprocity has an instability is made.
5. **Other trust boundaries.** A hard transfer cutoff can destroy PSD of static pairing repulsion (G3X / finite-band manuscript); a symmetric two-factor cutoff does not. An arbitrary independent weight sequence \(b_0=1,b_{n>0}=0\) at soft transfer gives the canceled quotient \(2K_1\), as in the case-(e) relaxed counterexample, and exceeds one for \(J/(2\pi\tau)^2>1/2\) sufficiently close to the soft limit. It is not a physical self-consistent branch and cannot settle Q2. Finite inverse moment is a condition for finite bare static transfer, not a necessity theorem for every limiting cancellation scheme: \(d\mathfrak m=E\,1_{(0,1)}dE\) has finite moment but infinite \(K_0\); \(E^{-1}1_{(1,\infty)}dE\) gives the opposite case. Reality and high-frequency normalization are also substantive: with a subtracted dispersion relation the entire addition \(az^2\), \(a\ne0\) real, carries zero boundary loss but is not an instantaneous constant or finite Lorentzian decrement. No extension to vertex corrections, nonlinear thermodynamics, general sign-indefinite instantaneous attraction, or unrestricted off-shell moment projection is claimed.
